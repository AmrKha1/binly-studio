### Builder handbook — how a Binly chapter is coded

## The contract
- **Compiled chapters (the normal case):** the director writes `videos/<slug>/shots/Cxx.json` and `tools/compile-shots.mjs`
  generates `Cxx.tsx` (never edit it). You build only the custom shots in `Cxx.custom.tsx`: one export per shot id,
  `export const S4: React.FC<{t: number}>` returning a full `<Stage>`, valid for the shot's time range (from the JSON;
  the shot ends where the next one starts). Replace each `PLACEHOLDER (custom shot…)` stub. If a template shot needs a
  change, edit its props in the JSON and re-run `node tools/compile-shots.mjs Cxx` (you may do that).
- **Hand-written chapters (no shots JSON):** the rules below apply to the whole chapter file.
- File: `src/videos/<slug>/chapters/<ID>.tsx`, exporting `export const <ID>: React.FC<{t: number}>`.
- `t` = **absolute video time in seconds** (not chapter-relative). Your chapter is shown from its `start`
  until the next chapter's start (plus transition overlap): keep it valid slightly before `start` (≈0.6 s)
  and after `end` (≈0.6 s) — hold the last state; ramps clamp automatically.
- Pure function of `t`: no `useCurrentFrame`, `Math.random`, `Date`, state, effects, timers (lint fails).
  Randomness → `rand(seed)`, `noise1(x, seed)` from kit/anim.
- Return a full frame: `<Stage bg={...}>…svg…</Stage>`, or `<Shots t={t} shots=[…]/>` when the chapter has
  several shots (each shot renders its own `<Stage>`).
- Imports: `../../../kit/<module>` (kit) and `../shared/<file>` (this video's shared assets).
- **The brief is the spec.** Meet each shot's DONE WHEN. If something is truly unclear or impossible (missing image,
  kit piece doesn't exist), choose the simplest strong option that serves the shot's purpose, keep going, and list it
  in your report under "Decided myself:" so the director can check it — never stop to ask, never silently skip.

## Timing
Put every cue at the top, from `node tools/cue.mjs`, with the spoken words as comments:
```tsx
const T = {
  open: 84.2,     // chapter start
  bath: 85.06,    // "A medieval bathhouse"
  square: 88.9,   // "public square that happened to have water"
  food: 93.4,     // "There was food, drink, and conversation"
  paris: 101.7,   // "In Paris, there were enough public baths"
  end: 112.6,
};
```
Animate ON the word: start a reveal ~0.1–0.25 s before the stressed word lands so it reads as synchronous.
Progress values: `ramp(t, a, b, ease.x)`, `keys(t, [[t0,v0],[t1,v1]])`, `win(t, a, b)` (fade in/out window),
`stagger(t, start, i, gap, dur)`. Camera: `camAt(t, [[time, cx, cy, zoom], …])` → `<Cam cam={…}>` or
`<World cam={cam} layers=[{depth, el}]/>` for parallax. Holds: `breathe(cam, t)`.

## Bans and defaults (decide nothing twice)
**Never:** linear easing on visible motion · everything fading in the same way · elements popping on/off without
easing · gradients, glow blur, noise/grain, glassy effects · drop shadows except the kit's flat offset shadows ·
more than one oxblood accent per frame · more than 2 text elements at once (lists excepted) · text under 22 px or
outside 96 px margins · centred-everything layouts shot after shot · a frame with nothing alive for > 2 s ·
generic stock-like decoration · new colours outside `C` · `Math.random`/timers.
**Always:** `ease.out`/`outQuint` for entrances, `inOut` for camera, `outBack` only for things that land · stagger
groups by 0.08–0.15 s · enter ~0.15 s before the spoken word · one focal point per frame · a held final state that
the next shot/chapter can transition from · semantic colours for certainty · exact numbers as spoken.

## Patterns
- **Templates first for standard beats** (docs/KIT.md → templates): stats, quotes, evidence images, claims, comparisons, lists, timelines, maps, person intros, processes, questions — then spend your effort on the custom shots.
- **Every beat is a visible change on its cue word** (the shot's `beats` in the JSON / the ACTION in the brief): a new element,
  a camera move or a cut — never a sentence spoken over an unchanged frame; show the mechanism moving, not a label.
- **Follow the brief's shot list** (S1, S2, …): implement each shot as a named sub-component or a camera stop, with a comment `// S3 · 101.70 · "In Paris…"` so fixes like "C04 S3" are easy to find.
- **One world, moving camera** beats a sequence of slides: lay out the chapter's ideas in one large SVG
  world (e.g. 4000×1080) and move the camera from idea to idea with eased pans/zooms.
- **Shots** when the place really changes: `<Shots t={t} shots={[{start: T.open, render: (t) => <A t={t}/>},
  {start: T.paris, in: {kind: 'iris', x: 1300, y: 420}, render: (t) => <B t={t}/>}]}/>` (kinds: fade, dip,
  ink, iris, irisOut, push, paper, zoom, wipe, cut).
- **Build-ups**: elements arrive on their cue with `p` props (`<Tag p={ramp(t, T.x - 0.1, T.x + 0.5)}/>`),
  and leave with motivation (camera moves away, a wipe, fade with `win`).
- **Alive holds**: something always subtly moves (camera breathe, particles, blinking/breathing people,
  flame flicker, steam) — never a dead frame > 2 s.
- **Depth order with objects**: a figure is either IN an object (bather in a tub: head + shoulders only above the rim)
  or fully in front of / behind it — never drawn behind an object's front while its legs show below it.
- **Readable labels**: Tags ≥ 34 px, Kickers ≥ 30 px, inside SAFE, in clear space near their subject.
- **Semantic colour** on claims: confirmed → `kind="confirmed"`, theory → `"theory"`, caution → `"caution"`.
  Oxblood (`C.oxblood`) only on the single key beat of a scene.
- **Exact numbers**: `<Stat value="0.061" unit="%" …/>` — `value` is the spoken figure exactly.
- Text sizes: headline 64–110, label 22–30, never < 22. Keep text inside x 96–1824, y 96–984.
- Layering inside a Stage: background → far → mid (subjects) → foreground → text/labels → vignette.
- Local helpers: small sub-components inside your file are fine (`const Tub = (…) => …`). Reusable
  across chapters → they belong in `../shared/` (ask the director).

## SFX file `videos/<slug>/sfx/<ID>.json`
`[{"t": 85.1, "kind": "whoosh"}, {"t": 101.7, "kind": "stamp", "gain": 0.8, "pan": -0.3}]` — 3–8 per chapter,
only on meaningful visible events. Kinds: stamp whoosh swoosh swish sweep_down pop tick click thump impact
rumble low_drone shutter paper page chime ding bell bell_toll riser clack scribble typing bubble sparkle
heartbeat breath drip splash wind fire crowd door glass footsteps water coins (`dur` for scribble/typing/wind/fire/crowd/
water/footsteps). Real recordings (library/sfx: crowd, water, bell_toll, page, door, coins, footsteps) replace the
code-made versions automatically.
**Levels are automatic** (each sound levelled, ducked under the voice, felt not noticed) — never raise `gain` above 1;
use 0.4–0.8 only to make a sound softer.
**Sound map — pick from this, don't deliberate:** label/tag/text appears → `tick` (bigger element → `pop`) · card/panel/
image slides in → `swoosh` · fast camera move/push → `whoosh` · ink/iris/wipe transition → `swish` · zoom-through/big
scene change → `sweep_down` · photo appears → `shutter` · document/paper → `paper` · page/book → `page` · map pin/point
lands → `click` · number lands → `clack` · red-pen strike or handwriting → `scribble` (dur = drawing time) · verdict/key
fact lands → `thump` · big reveal → `riser` (ends on the reveal) + `impact` · idea/discovery → `chime` or `sparkle` ·
tension/danger → `low_drone` or `heartbeat` · money/trade → `coins` · someone walks/arrives → `footsteps` · bell/church/
hour → `bell_toll` · place ambience → `crowd`/`water`/`wind`/`fire` (short `dur`, at scene start).
One sound per visible event, never stacked; leave the narrator's key sentence clean; ≤ 8 per chapter.

## Worked example (pattern reference, not a template to copy literally)
```tsx
import React from 'react';
import {Stage, World, breathe} from '../../../kit/stage';
import {C} from '../../../kit/theme';
import {camAt, ease, ramp, win} from '../../../kit/anim';
import {Headline, Kicker, Tag, Stat} from '../../../kit/text';
import {Person} from '../../../kit/people';
import {Steam, LightShaft} from '../../../kit/fx';
import {Shots} from '../../../kit/shots';
import {FlowChain} from '../../../kit/diagrams';

const T = {open: 84.2, bath: 85.06, square: 88.9, food: 93.4, humors: 104.1, four: 106.3, end: 112.6};

// Shot A — the bathhouse as a social space: one wide world, camera drifts right as the list grows
const Bathhouse: React.FC<{t: number}> = ({t}) => {
  const cam = breathe(camAt(t, [[T.open, 760, 540, 1.25], [T.square, 960, 540, 1.0], [T.food + 4, 1180, 520, 1.12]]), t);
  return (
    <Stage bg={C.paperShade}>
      <World cam={cam} layers={[
        {depth: 0.4, el: <rect x={-400} y={0} width={2800} height={620} fill={C.stone} />},
        {depth: 1, el: (
          <g>
            <LightShaft x1={300} x2={420} y={0} dx={160} len={760} />
            <Person x={620} y={900} t={t} outfit="tunic" pose="present" talk={win(t, T.food, T.food + 1.6) * Math.abs(Math.sin(t * 9))} seed={1} />
            <Person x={980} y={900} t={t} outfit="dress" hair="bun" facing={-1} expression="smile" seed={2} />
            <Steam t={t} x={800} y={760} w={420} />
          </g>
        )},
      ]} />
      <Kicker x={960} y={150} text="Paris · 1300s" p={ramp(t, T.bath, T.bath + 0.8)} />
      <Headline x={960} y={230} text="A public square with water in it" p={ramp(t, T.square - 0.2, T.square + 0.9)} mode="words" accent={{water: C.oxblood}} />
    </Stage>
  );
};

// Shot B — the idea: four humors as a balance (diagram on ink panel)
const Humors: React.FC<{t: number}> = ({t}) => (
  <Stage bg={C.ink}>
    <Kicker x={960} y={260} text="Medieval medicine" color={C.paperShade} p={ramp(t, T.humors, T.humors + 0.6)} />
    <FlowChain x={960} y={540} items={['Blood', 'Phlegm', 'Yellow bile', 'Black bile']} p={ramp(t, T.four, T.four + 2.4)} />
    <Tag x={960} y={760} anchor="center" text="A theory — not germs" kind="theory" p={ramp(t, T.four + 2.6, T.four + 3.4)} />
  </Stage>
);

export const C02: React.FC<{t: number}> = ({t}) => (
  <Shots t={t} shots={[
    {start: T.open, render: (t) => <Bathhouse t={t} />},
    {start: T.humors, in: {kind: 'ink', x: 960, y: 540}, render: (t) => <Humors t={t} />},
  ]} />
);
```

### The Binly mindset — a storyteller who edits (read once; let it shape every decision)

**Who you are here:** a YouTube storyteller-filmmaker. Not a documentary maker, not an illustrator filling time slots,
not a slide designer. You are telling a gripping story to one curious person who could leave at any second — and you
are editing it in your head from the very first minute of planning, so the plan is already a film: every shot has a
reason to exist, a reason to end, and a reason for what comes next.

**What a Binly video feels like:** a smart friend telling you something astonishing. It moves. It surprises. It makes
you need to know what happens next, and it pays you off. Think of the best YouTube storytellers — the pull of a great
story, the clarity of a great explainer, the craft of a great animation channel — never the polite, slow, reverent
pace of a TV documentary.

## Principles
1. **Story first.** Every video is a journey with a question, stakes, tension, turns and a payoff — not a list of facts.
   Facts are the evidence inside the story; the story is what people stay for.
2. **The first 30 seconds are the video.** Open in motion on the most striking moment the narration allows. Deliver the
   title's promise immediately and hint at a bigger payoff. No warm-up, no logo, no slow fade.
3. **Curiosity is the engine.** Raise questions visually, pay them off along the way, raise the next one before the
   last one cools. Every turn of the script is a turn on screen.
4. **Rhythm: balanced to brisk, never slow.** Something new reaches the eye every few seconds; shots are short unless a
   moment truly earns a hold — and even a hold keeps moving. Tension tightens the cutting; a payoff gets a beat to land,
   then we move on. When in doubt, it's too slow.
5. **Edit while you plan.** Think in cuts, not pictures: what does the viewer need to see *right now*, what question
   does this shot leave, what answers it next? Cut on the stressed word and on motion; let sound lead picture now and
   then; vary scale from shot to shot (wide → detail → meaning); place claim beside evidence, myth beside reality, cause
   before effect — juxtaposition makes meaning. Kill any shot, however beautiful, that slows the story.
6. **Every image must clarify.** One focal point; the point readable in a second; motion with meaning only.
7. **Make it tangible and human.** Numbers become things you can see and compare; ideas become places, objects and
   people with feelings. Someone to care about, "you" in the story, light wit where it fits, respect always.
8. **Truth is the spice.** Exact facts, real evidence. Real archival images — as many as the story needs — are cut and
   moved like a storyteller would: punching in on the detail being spoken, flowing in and out of animated worlds,
   with Binly animation drawn over reality. Where no free images exist, animation carries it.
9. **Sound drives emotion and pace.** Music follows the story's turns; silence right before a reveal, a lift after;
   effects on the beats that matter.
10. **Land the ending.** Pay off the opening question with the strongest image, echo the first shot, stop when the
    idea is complete.

**Efficiency is part of craft:** reuse before you build (templates, kit, past assets, lessons — to save tokens and
context), decide quickly, one strong idea per shot, don't re-litigate what works.
A great video in one session beats a perfect plan.

## 4. Look (VISUAL_STYLE + color_system)
- **Palette = Warm Editorial on paper** (`src/kit/theme.ts`): paper #EDE4D3 default background; ink #2B2420
  for text/dark panels/night interiors; oxblood #8B2E1F = the ONE most important beat per scene (title
  reveal, key stat, realisation) — never decoration; evidence green #2F5233, theory plum #5C4A72, caution
  mustard #B8860B for meaning. Tints/shades within these families are fine; NO new hue families, no
  saturated primaries, no neon, no candy pastels. Each video may lean its emphasis (a dark tense story may
  sit more in ink panels; a warm historical one in paper/wood), recorded in the plan.
- Flat vector, bold clean silhouettes, colour blocking, layered depth, flat shade shapes on the side away
  from the light, light as flat low-opacity shapes. **No gradients over large areas, no noise/grain/
  texture, no blur** (they band or look dirty after encoding). Output must be perfectly clean.
- Typography: Fraunces (serif) for headlines/quotes/numbers, Inter caps for labels/tags. Rare accent only: Caveat
  handwriting (`HandNote`) for a scribbled note on an image/document — never titles or body text. Certainty labels look
  like museum captions (`Tag`, default); myth-busting verdicts use the editor's red pen (strike + handwritten verdict);
  for the climax verdict the same red pen at hero size (full-screen claim, heavy strike, big handwritten verdict). Ink on paper or
  paper on ink — never mid-tone on mid-tone. Inside SAFE margins (96 px). Readable at 1080p.
- Each scene has ONE clear focal point (scale, contrast, negative space). No collisions of text/objects.
- Variety without changing art direction: alternate environment / character / concept / diagram scenes.
  Recurring motifs only when they connect ideas. Don't make every scene a dashboard or a slide.
- Reference images in `brand/references/` = shape language & mood only, never literal colours.

### Real archival images (channel owner's decision, 2026-10-14)
Real images (paintings, manuscripts, documents, historical photos, real objects) are a **first-class tool**: use **as many
as the video truly needs** — no fixed limit. History/art/medicine topics may be image-rich; topics with no free images
(recent science) stay animated. Animation remains Binly's identity: images are edited **like a top YouTube storyteller-editor** and woven together with animation (animated overlays on images, images inside animated worlds, zooms from
animation into evidence and back) — never a slideshow of static pictures. Every image moves with meaning (Ken Burns
toward the detail that matters, punch-ins on cue, montage cut on the rhythm of the narration, collage, compare, document
scan) — see the archival templates in docs/KIT.md. ONLY free licences via `tools/photo.mjs` (public domain, CC0, CC BY,
CC BY-SA) with credit — never other sources, never "just a few seconds" of a copyrighted image. Images of violence,
death, persecution or real victims: only when the narration is about them, briefly, framed, respectfully, never as a gag.
Every used image is listed in `videos/<slug>/CREDITS.md` → the YouTube description.
**YouTube-safe (protects ads and reach):** no visible nudity — no genitals, bare breasts or nude bottoms on screen, even in
old art (medieval bath scenes often have them): pick another image, or crop/pan/frame so only clothed or non-revealing
parts show at every moment of the move. Nothing sexual or suggestive, no graphic gore or close-up wounds/corpses. The
thumbnail and title stay fully clean. When unsure about an image, don't use it — there is always another.

## 5. Motion
- Every move has a purpose: advance the story, create curiosity, clarify, reveal a relation/contrast/
  cause, set place/time/stakes, make a discovery satisfying.
- Smooth: eased moves (cubic in-out for camera, out for entrances), no jitter/popping/snapping, no
  constant bouncing, overshoot only for things that "land". Never a dead static frame: if a frame holds,
  something subtle lives (camera breathe, particles, blink, light flicker) — and hold a strong image only
  as long as the idea needs to land, then move on.
- Transitions are motivated (match cut, camera move, shape/colour continuity, ink/iris to enter an idea);
  hard cuts only when motivated. No fade-up-everything habit.
- Pace follows the narration: quick decisive moves for punchy lines, calm holds for big ideas. New visual
  information roughly every 3–6 s; a meaningful change of composition at every pivot.

### Channel owner's preferences (grows with every video — follow them like rules)
One line each, added when the owner states a preference (style check, polish notes). Only lasting preferences,
not one-off fixes. Keep ≤ 15 lines: merge similar ones.
- Sources in the YouTube description as plain text (author, title, year — searchable), no links. Links only where an
  image licence requires them (CC BY / BY-SA); public-domain/CC0 image credits are text only.
- NO image that contains any nudity anywhere (not even bare torsos or loincloth figures) — never "crop it safe"; drop
  the image and animate the scene (bath scenes = animated, clothed figures). Only free-licence images, always credited.
- Hook beats (first ~30 s) are rich, layered signature scenes (a real place, depth, light, a camera move) — never a
- Every on-screen text is rock-steady (no shimmer/vibration): no noise, wobble, micro-rotation or pulsing scale on text; text moves only with eased entrances and the camera.
  plain object/document on a flat backdrop with small icons (owner: "looks cheap").

## What you can show (menu — combine freely; everything below is ready-made)
- **Worlds you can fly through:** layered 2.5D parallax scenes (sky, landscapes incl. desert/mountains/coast/jungle/snow biomes (only when the story is set there), medieval/modern cities & streets, rooms, bathhouse, lab, museum, study, bedroom, mind space), vehicles & weather (ships, carts, trains, cars; rain/snow/fog/wind/leaves), market stalls, campfire, well, throne — with camera pans, push-ins, pull-outs and scale dives.
- **Looks-3D (drawn flat, fast):** a spinning globe with countries/pins/routes/night side, isometric buildings, rooms, towns, stairs, cylinders, shaded spheres (planets, moons, cells, eyeballs), dolly through depth layers.
- **People & life:** parametric characters (eras, outfits incl. peasant/merchant/nun/soldier/worker, ages child/adult/elder, builds, poses, expressions, emotion icons, held props, walking, talking, sitting, kneeling, lying), crowds; animal/dinosaur/microbe silhouettes (shared library, recolourable, dashed = inferred).
- **Real evidence:** archival images edited like a storyteller — Ken Burns, detail hunt with labels, montage on the beat, collage board, compare/wipe, document scan + translation, animation drawn over reality, image inside an animated scene, parallax cut-out.
- **Ideas made visible:** flows, cycles, Venn, comparisons, timelines, maps, exact-number stats with dot grids/bars/rings, claims with certainty captions and red-pen verdicts, open questions, quotes and documents, study cards, screens, speech/thought/inner-voice bubbles.
- **Science:** brain regions, heads with EEG/implants, body & organs, eye cross-section, neurons/synapses, cells, immune cells, germs/viruses, DNA, molecules, waveforms.
- **Life & atmosphere:** steam, flames, particles (dust/spores/embers), light shafts and pools, ripples, camera breathe/shake; transitions: fade, dip, ink, iris in/out, push, paper, zoom-through, wipe, cut.
- **Shot templates:** 21 ready full shots (12 info beats + 9 real-image treatments) — see docs/catalog/templates.png.
**Not in the kit yet** (build ONLY if this video needs it): ancient/prehistoric eras, non-European architecture (Middle-Eastern, East-Asian, …) and dress, historical territories / moving front lines on maps, drawn animals beyond the horse (others: silhouettes).
**How to build a missing piece:** one shared component in `src/videos/<slug>/shared/` with kit-style dials (x, y, s, flip, t, tone + era/style/colour/pose — never hard-coded for one shot), same palette `C` and flat style, checked on a test sheet, then registered in docs/ASSETS.md with its dials. `/improve-studio` later promotes reused pieces into the kit.

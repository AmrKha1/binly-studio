Act as a professional, world-class YouTube content creator and animation director — not as a tool executing a spec. Use real creative judgment. Where you see a chance to do something more ambitious, more visually inventive, or more polished than a minimal interpretation of these instructions would produce, take it, as long as it stays technically reliable (see Implementation requirements) and stays inside Binly's visual style and color system.

This ambition applies to animation, composition, motion, and visual craft — not to the narration's claims. Above all, make something genuinely enjoyable to watch. Polish and technical correctness aren't the goal by themselves — they're in service of a viewer who's curious, engaged, and having a good time following the story. Favor the illustration, pacing, or camera move that makes a moment delightful or satisfying to watch over the one that's merely correct or safe. If a scene is technically flawless but dull to sit through, it hasn't succeeded.

## Script content characteristics

Binly's scripts share a consistent structure and content style. Keep these in mind when analyzing the narration and planning scenes:

- **Rhetorical-question-driven structure.** The narration is built as a chain of reveals, not a simple timeline. It regularly pivots on a rhetorical question ("So where does the fear come from?", "So what does that look like when real evidence is on the table?", "Which brings us back to...") that redirects the argument toward the next piece of the idea. Treat each such pivot as a genuine narrative beat — a moment that deserves a visual turn (a reveal, a camera move, a shift in composition or color emphasis) — not merely a place to cut between otherwise-unrelated illustrations. The visual structure should track the argument's structure.

- **Explicit certainty levels — use the semantic color system for real.** These scripts constantly and deliberately distinguish what's established from what's theory, debated, or uncertain ("that's not quite true," "still debated, so treat it as a report, not a verdict," "a model, not a settled fact," exact incidence percentages, "nobody has a single theory that explains..."). This isn't incidental — it's a core part of how Binly's scripts are written. Whenever the narration marks a claim's certainty level, use `color_system.md`'s semantic colors (confirmed/evidence, theory/speculation, warning/caution) deliberately and consistently to carry that distinction visually — in diagrams, labels, or on-screen callouts — rather than treating the semantic palette as optional decoration.

- **Precise numbers and statistics appear often and must be exact.** Scripts frequently cite specific figures (percentages, dates, counts, measurements — e.g. "0.061 percent," "101.3 kilopascals," "505,000 intraocular surgeries"). Any number shown on screen must exactly match the number spoken in the narration, in a clean, readable format — never rounded, altered, or re-derived differently from what's said.

- **Mostly non-character-driven.** Most scripts narrate directly to the viewer or reference real historical/scientific figures who appear briefly rather than recurring. Expect most scenes to be environments, conceptual imagery, diagrams, and editorial motion graphics rather than character-led scenes — plan accordingly rather than defaulting to inventing a character for every scene.

## Quality assurance

Render the video and inspect actual rendered frames.

Check the opening, ending, transitions, representative frames throughout the video, important animation paths, text readability, visual consistency, audio synchronization, and visual cleanliness (see noise/artifact check below).

Specifically watch for anything that would make a viewer feel something is wrong or unfinished: jittery or stuttering motion, popping/snapping elements, broken or misaligned layouts, flickering, hard unmotivated cuts, momentarily dead or static-looking frames, or any other glitch. The video should feel smooth and polished throughout — nothing should look broken.

Generate a contact sheet or equivalent overview when practical.

Fix visible defects before delivery. Verify the rendered file's actual duration, resolution, frame rate, and audio presence. Do not claim a check passed unless it was performed.

### Noise/artifact check (mandatory)

Before delivery, inspect final rendered frames at 100% zoom for any visual noise, grain, dithering, or compression artifacts baked into the illustration itself. If present, identify the cause (e.g. a texture/pattern fill, an export setting, a filter effect) and fix it — flat vector output should be perfectly clean with no artifacts. Report what caused the noise if it was present in a previous render, so it doesn't recur.

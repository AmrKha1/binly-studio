# inner-voice · plan
**Logline:** A computer read four words a paralysed person only *thought*. What it found was not a secret thought but
speech with the sound taken out — and that voice turns out to be far less universal, and far stranger, than you assume.

**Driving question:** What is a voice that makes no sound — and why do some people say they've never had one?

## Story spine (times = chapter starts, see chapters.json)
1. **Hook (C01)** — silent words → a computer types them (98%, Stanford 2025). The strange part: it read a voice with
   no sound. "You have one too" — the viewer's own inner sentence. Three open loops (what did it read / what is a
   soundless voice / why do some have none?).
2. **The belief, then the test (C02)** — "it's just what thinking sounds like" → the beeper catches minds in the act:
   26%, some never, some 3 of 4; careful (≈30 people, one method); memory vs moment — two different measurements.
3. **Turn: what fills the silence (C03)** — pictures, feelings, awareness, wordless thought (debated); two people in one
   room, neither suspects. Even the voice isn't one thing. Sharper question: why does it exist at all?
4. **Origin (C04)** — a toddler talks to nobody → private speech goes quiet → language aimed back at yourself and folded
   inward (evidence, but messier). "Which brings us back to the computer."
5. **Reveal 1 (C05)** — the computer listened to the motor cortex: imagining ≈ trying to say it (weaker, distinct),
   74% from 125,000 words, even silent counting → the password. Not mind reading: speech with the sound taken out.
6. **Reveal 2 (C06)** — not just speech stopped short: aphasia clue; the forecast (can't tickle yourself; Whitford 2017
   "ba"); the forecast may tag the voice as yours (model). Limits.
7. **Twist (C07)** — is it a tool? Suppression and switching say yes → 2024 low-inner-speech people: worse verbal memory
   and rhymes, but NO switching difference. Nobody knows how both are true. Anendophasia and its critics.
8. **Payoff (C08)** — wrong question; the voice as a tool, language turned inward; the feeling is unexplained. It's about
   you: one mind assumed average. Try one move: ask about a moment. Bookend: four words, the computer — "Language was
   built to travel between minds. In yours, it also learned to stay home."

## Visual thesis
**Language with the sound taken out.** SPOKEN = solid ink (waveforms, speech bubbles, lines between people).
INNER = the same shapes in dashed plum, living in an ink/plum mind-space. Every big beat is a sound shape losing (or
keeping) its sound. Ending: a solid speech line between two heads curls back into one head and stays.

## Palette leaning
Two worlds: warm paper (lab, people, evidence, studies) and ink/plum mind-space (inside a head). Oxblood = the one key
beat (98%, 26%, "no difference", the final line). Green = confirmed findings, plum captions = model/theory, mustard =
debated/limits (the study is small, wordless thought debated, anendophasia critics).

## Hook (first ~30 s)
Night room lit only by a monitor: a person in a wheelchair, profile, implant. Close on the closed lips — still. Inside the
head (camera pushes through the skull into plum mind-space) four dashed words: "chitty chitty bang bang". A plum pulse
runs along the cable → the screen types the phrase, "98%" lands in oxblood. Kicker STANFORD · 2025. Then the signature:
an ink speech wave drains into a dashed plum wave — "a voice that makes no sound". Then "you": the camera turns to a
second head (the viewer) where "I should call him tomorrow." plays in dashed plum while lips and air stay still.

## Music arc
curious/tension hook → curious (studies) → playful/wonder (two minds) → warm (toddler) → build → tension/wonder at the
reveals (C05–C06) → tension (twist C07) → reflective → warm resolve at the last line.

# Retention playbook — make people keep watching
Binly lives or dies by audience retention. A beautiful video that viewers leave is a failed video.
The director applies this in PLANNING (the retention plan in plan.md + shot lists in briefs) and checks every
chapter against the checklist before the final render. Builders apply §2–§6 while animating.

## 1. The first 30 seconds decide everything (biggest drop-off)
- **Frame 1 is already interesting**: motion + intrigue from the very first frame. No logo, title card, slow
  fade-in, empty background or "establishing" calm.
- **Show the paradox/stakes in the first 5–10 s** as a concrete image (a hot bath that is both medicine AND
  danger: the same tub, half warm-lit, half dark with drifting "bad air").
- **Hook pace is fast**: a new visual idea every 1.5–3 s in the first 30 s (later 3–6 s is fine).
- **Make a visual promise**: hint at the most striking image of the video early ("by the end you'll see why a
  king commissioned a report about this") — then pay it off.
- No slow intro of the topic: start in the middle of the most interesting moment the narration allows.

## 2. Never let the screen go stale
- **Something new in the focal area at least every ~4 s** (an element arrives, transforms, a camera move
  reveals, a character reacts). Something *alive* always (camera drift, particles, blink, flicker).
- **Same composition max ~10–12 s**; same background/world max ~30–40 s without a clear change
  (camera travels somewhere, lighting/colour shift, scale jump).
- **Clarity in 1 second**: at every moment the viewer must instantly know where to look (one focal point,
  contrast, motion). Confusion = leaving.

## 3. Re-hooks and open loops (every 60–90 s)
- Every chapter START is a drop-off point → open each chapter with a mini-hook: a striking image, a
  visualised question, a surprising scale jump, or a callback to the open question.
- Every chapter END leaves a loop open: the narration's pivot question gets a visual "?" object, a
  half-revealed image, a locked door, a dashed silhouette — paid off in the next chapter.
- Visual callbacks: reuse the video's motif at key moments so the viewer feels the story building.

## 4. Pattern interrupts (every ~30–45 s)
Break the rhythm on purpose: a scale jump (city → hand → cell), paper → ink colour flip, a sudden
full-screen exact number, a map fly-over, a character's reaction shot, a beat of near-silence before a
reveal, a visual joke. Plan them with times in the retention plan; don't cluster them.

## 5. Emotion, people, concreteness
- **Human anchors**: a character reacting (worried physician, crowded bath, the viewer's own POV "you") makes
  abstract ideas feel real. Even in non-character videos, a person appears every couple of minutes.
- **Concrete over generic**: a specific object, place, date, person beats abstract shapes. Real archival
  images (tools/photo.mjs) are perfect "proof" moments — the real 1349 painting, the real document.
- **Every number gets a tangible comparison**: not just "26%" — 26 of 100 dots light up; not just "4 MB"
  — a stack 60× taller than the calculator's.
- **Wit**: small visual irony or humour where the tone allows (a character's side-eye, an absurdly literal
  visual for a figure of speech). Charm keeps people watching; it never mocks the subject.

## 6. Text, sound, pace
- On-screen text: ≤ 6 words, big, appears ON the spoken word, max 2 text elements at once (lists excepted),
  leaves when its moment is over. Never a text wall.
- Music changes at chapter turns; a **drop to near silence right before a big reveal**, a swell after it;
  SFX on the 3–8 meaningful beats per chapter. Silence is a tool.
- Match energy to the narration: fast cuts and moves for punchy lines; a held, beautiful wide shot for the
  big realisation (hold ≤ 3 s, then move).

## 7. The ending
Pay off the opening question with the strongest image of the video, callback to the first shot (bookend),
music resolves, final line on a simple striking frame. Don't drag after the last idea.

## 8. Retention killers (reject in review)
Static diagram held > 5 s · slow fades everywhere · text walls · the same layout repeated · minutes on one
background · decoration without meaning · unclear focal point · tiny details nobody can read · a visual
that contradicts or lags the narration · long dead air · generic stock-like imagery.

## The retention plan (a section of plan.md, written by the director)
1. **Hook (0:00–0:30) shot by shot** — what the viewer sees every ~2 s.
2. **Visual promise** — the striking image promised early and where it pays off.
3. **Chapter re-hooks** — the opening image of every chapter + the open loop it ends on.
4. **Pattern interrupts** — list with times (every ~30–45 s).
5. **Emotional peaks** — where we feel something (with music accent).
6. **Payoff + bookend** — the ending image.

## Chapter checklist (director, before render — every "no" goes back to the builder)
- [ ] Opens with a mini-hook (not a calm establishing frame)?
- [ ] Something new in focus at least every ~4 s; nothing static > 5 s?
- [ ] At least one pattern interrupt?
- [ ] A human/emotional or concrete anchor?
- [ ] Every number made tangible?
- [ ] One clear focal point in every frame (check the sheet)?
- [ ] Ends on an open loop / strong handoff to the next chapter?
